## 02. Visual Identity

### Official logo

Use the supplied official ProFox logo artwork as the master asset.

- Preserve its original proportions.
- Keep generous clear space around the mark.
- Do not stretch or skew it.
- Do not add shadows, outlines or unapproved effects.
- Do not recolor the supplied artwork casually.
- If an alternate logo version is required, create and approve it as a formal brand asset.
- Place the logo only on backgrounds with strong contrast and readability.
- Use the logo as a signature, not as wallpaper.

### Official brand colors

- **Primary Navy Blue:** `#000080`
- **Secondary Red:** `#FF0E0E`

Navy is the authority color. Use it for major headings, structural elements, premium backgrounds and high-trust moments.

Red is the action color. Use it for emphasis, directional cues, rules, highlights and selected calls to action.

White and restrained neutrals support the system; they do not replace the official brand colors.

### Color hierarchy

1. Use White or Navy as the primary visual field.
2. Navy (`#000080`) is the strongest structural brand signal.
3. Use Red (`#FF0E0E`) for emphasis and action.
4. Keep Red restrained so every red element feels intentional.
5. Use neutral dark body text for readability.
6. Do not introduce random brand colors.

### Visual discipline

- One dominant idea per page, banner or social visual.
- Use generous whitespace. Premium does not mean filling every empty area.
- Keep typography modern, clean and consistent.
- Use the logo as a signature, not wallpaper.
- A well-designed asset should still feel like ProFox even if the logo is temporarily hidden.

### Design rule

> **Navy leads. Red directs.**

Use Navy to create authority and structure. Use Red to guide attention and action. If everything is emphasized, nothing is emphasized.

## 05. Messaging System

Every channel should use the same hierarchy. The shorter the available space, the higher in the hierarchy the message should stay.

### Level 1 — Brand idea

**From site to system.**

Use on master banners, homepage brand sections, brand films, pitch openers and profile headers.

### Level 2 — Positioning

**We design the digital experiences and systems that move business forward.**

Use when one sentence is needed to explain the company.

### Level 3 — Service family

**Websites · Applications · Automation**

Use in bios, footers, short descriptors and visual lockups.

### Level 4 — Branded services

**ProFox Web · ProFox Apps · ProFox Flow**

Use for navigation, service campaigns, case studies and sales materials.

### Level 5 — Functional clarity

**Website Design & Development · Custom Web Application Development · Email Marketing & Business Automation**

Use for SEO, proposals, procurement, detailed pages and contexts where clarity outranks elegance.

### Compression rule

Do not solve a small space by shrinking more words into it. Move upward in the hierarchy. A banner may need only the brand idea; a proposal may use all five levels.

## 08. Brand Voice

### How ProFox sounds

The voice should feel:

- **Calm**
- **Intelligent**
- **Concise**
- **Certain**
- **Human**

Confidence comes from clarity, not inflated adjectives.

| Principle | Do | Do not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clear | Say what changes for the customer. | Stack adjectives. |
| Concise | Prefer one strong sentence. | Explain the whole company in every headline. |
| Human | Write like an expert speaking to a smart client. | Use corporate filler. |
| Specific | Name the action, system or outcome. | Use “solutions” when a clearer noun exists. |
| Confident | State the point without begging for attention. | Use hype, excessive punctuation or fake urgency. |
| Connected | Show how parts work together. | Present every service as an unrelated silo. |

### Preferred sentence patterns

- Action → outcome: “Design the journey around what customers need to do next.”
- Tension → resolution: “Software should fit the business. Not the other way around.”
- Simple contrast: “Less repetition. More momentum.”
- Belief: “The best digital experiences do not work alone.”
- Capability with purpose: “Automation that keeps the journey moving.”

### Words to favor

build · design · connect · move · fit · work · journey · system · experience · business · customer · flow · clarity · action · momentum

### Use sparingly / avoid

innovative · cutting-edge · world-class · revolutionary · game-changing · one-stop solution · digital transformation partner · best-in-class · synergy · 360-degree solutions · affordable excellence

## 12. Trust & Conversion

### Trust without clutter

Do not force proof into the master tagline. Brand promise and evidence are different layers.

### CTA hierarchy

- Primary: **Start a project**
- Secondary: **See our work**
- Consultative: **Book a strategy call**
- Case study: **See how it works**
- Service exploration: **Explore ProFox Web / Apps / Flow**

### Trust hierarchy

1. Proof of work — strong portfolio and case studies.
2. Proof of outcome — measurable business results where available and verifiable.
3. Proof of people — visible founder/team expertise and professional communication.
4. Proof of process — clear discovery, design, development, QA and handover standards.
5. Proof of legitimacy — current business registration, policies, contact information and commercial documents.
6. Proof of consistency — the same identity and message wherever a prospect encounters ProFox.

### Numbers and badges

Only publish ratings, project counts, client counts, certifications, registrations, awards or performance claims that are current and can be substantiated. Keep them in proof modules, not inside the permanent brand line.

Never invent or retain stale proof merely because it improves conversion.

## 13. Verbal + Visual Identity

The brand should feel as disciplined visually as it sounds verbally.

- **Hierarchy:** One dominant message per visual. Supporting copy should be visibly secondary.
- **Color:** Navy `#000080` is structural authority. Red `#FF0E0E` is accent/action. Use white and restrained neutrals for clarity and contrast.
- **Typography:** Use a modern sans-serif system consistently. Headings should be bold, concise and high-contrast. Body copy must stay readable across digital and print.
- **Spacing:** Favor generous whitespace over filling every empty area.
- **Icons:** Use a consistent icon family and avoid decorative overload. Also obey `docs/UI_ICON_POLICY.md`.
- **Photography/graphics:** Prefer real digital experiences, interfaces, systems and human business moments. Avoid generic “team pointing at laptop” stock imagery where possible.
- **Service cues:** Web = surface/experience; Apps = modules/system; Flow = path/movement. Keep all three inside the same Navy + Red ProFox identity rather than assigning unrelated colors.

### Banner composition

1. ProFox brand mark/name.
2. **From site to system.** — dominant line.
3. **Web · Apps · Flow** — secondary signature.
4. Optional positioning line if space allows.
5. Website or CTA — small and clear, never competing with the headline.

## 16. Brand Governance

### Who must use this guide

- Founders and leadership
- Sales representatives
- Social-media managers
- Copywriters
- Designers
- Developers writing product copy
- Developers building or changing visual UI
- Freelancers and agencies
- Video editors and content creators
- Recruiters and employer-branding teams
- AI builders/coding agents acting on behalf of any of the above

### Change-control rules

1. Never rename a core service in public material without updating this document first.
2. Never introduce a new master tagline for a single campaign.
3. New service offers should fit under ProFox Web, ProFox Apps or ProFox Flow where logically possible.
4. If a future capability genuinely does not fit, add a new service family only after a brand-level decision — not because a campaign needs novelty.
5. Keep functional SEO names available underneath branded names so clarity is never sacrificed.
6. Before major legal registration, merchandising or large paid campaigns, check relevant trademark/name availability for branded service names and the master line in target markets.

### Annual brand review

- Check whether positioning still reflects what ProFox actually sells.
- Update proof points and outdated platform copy.
- Review service descriptions without casually replacing service names.
- Check new channels for visual and verbal consistency.
- Remove generic copy introduced over time.
- Keep the master line unless the company itself has materially changed category or strategy.

## Appendix — Brand Philosophy

The system separates permanent brand idea from campaign copy so ProFox does not change identity every time marketing needs a fresh headline.

It also separates branded service names from functional service names. Branded names create memory; functional names preserve clarity for search, proposals and procurement.

Trust is separate from hype. Trust should come from work, outcomes, people, process, legitimacy and consistency — not adjectives such as “world-class” or “best.”

The goal is to make the brand easy to remember, easy to recognize and hard to dilute.

### Naming safeguard

Before formal trademark registration or major international campaigns, run appropriate legal/trademark availability checks for **ProFox Web**, **ProFox Apps**, **ProFox Flow** and **From site to system.** in the relevant markets.

2. Do not invent replacement master positioning or service-family names.
